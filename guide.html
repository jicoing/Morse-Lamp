Morse Lamp Guide

Morse Lamp turns a short sentence into Morse code and sends it as tones, a flashing screen or the phone torch. It can also listen to you tap a message and decode it.

Open Morse Lamp

Send a message

  1. Open the Send tab and type a sentence of up to 60 characters. The dots and dashes appear as you type.
  2. Choose the outputs. Sound plays a tone, Screen flash turns the whole screen white for each signal, and Camera torch flashes the LED on phones that allow it.
  3. Set Speed in words per minute and Pitch in hertz. Start at 8–12 WPM if the person receiving it is a beginner.
  4. Press Transmit. The lamp on the page and the current dot or dash light up in time with the signal.
  5. Tap the screen, press Stop or press Esc to cancel.

Letters, numbers and the marks . , ? ! / - ' : @ are supported. Anything else is skipped.

Decode by tapping

Decode mode turns your taps into text. Use it to practise or to read a message someone is sending you by sound or light.

  1. Open the Decode tab and set Speed to match the sender. The app judges your taps against that speed.
  2. Press and hold the pad (or hold Space) for as long as the signal lasts. A short press is a dot and a long press is a dash.
  3. Pause briefly between letters. The app finishes the letter and shows it in the decoded box.
  4. Pause longer between words. The app adds a space.
  5. Use Backspace to remove the last character, Clear to start over, or Copy text to keep the result. Open the chart in the tab if you forget a letter.

That is SOS: three short taps, three long taps, three short taps. A letter the app cannot match shows as ?.

How the timing works

Everything is measured in one unit, which is the length of a dot. The unit in milliseconds is 1200 divided by the speed in WPM.

When decoding, the app counts a press shorter than 2 units as a dot and anything longer as a dash. A pause of 2 units or more ends the letter, and a pause of 5 units or more adds a space.

Good to know

  • The camera torch only works when the page is allowed to use the camera and the phone exposes its torch, which is mainly Chrome on Android. Where it is blocked, the app says so and keeps sending with sound and screen flash.
  • The screen flash blinks quickly. Lower the speed or switch it off if flashing light bothers you.
  • The app asks the phone to keep the screen on while it sends, if the phone allows it.
  • SOS is sent here as three separate letters with normal letter gaps, not as the run-together emergency signal.
  • Sound starts only after you press a button, because browsers block audio until you interact with the page.
